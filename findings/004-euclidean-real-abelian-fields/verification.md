## Exact claim under review

Assuming Hypotheses A, B and C of the [proof](proof.md#premises), which are Theorem 1.2, Proposition 2.1 and Lemma 9.2 of the family-029 manuscript, the draft proves: for every real abelian number field $`K`$ of degree at least two and every primitive unit $`\varepsilon`$, at least $`c_{K,\varepsilon}\,x/(\log x)^2`$ prime ideals of norm at most $`x`$ have residue field generated by $`-1`$ and $`\varepsilon`$ (Theorem 1); hence, by Graves's criterion, every such field of class number one is Euclidean (Theorem 2); and at least $`c\,x/(\log x)^2`$ primes up to $`x`$ have a Fibonacci primitive root (Corollary 3).

The claim is a lower bound of order $`x/(\log x)^2`$, not an asymptotic density. It concerns real abelian fields only. It does not assert anything about imaginary fields, about Euclidean ideal classes, or about a unit of norm $`+1`$ being itself a primitive root.

## Source premises: what was checked

The three premises were located and quoted from the manuscript text. Proposition 2.1 was confirmed to be base-free: its proof in the manuscript's Section 12 uses the Bombieri–Vinogradov theorem, the prime number theorem in progressions modulo $`M/c`$, two elementary sieve lemmas from Section 11, and a bilinear "marked Type II" estimate, Theorem 10.1 of the manuscript, which is imported from Theorem 3.1 of the family-011 manuscript *The Poisson–Dirichlet law for prime predecessors* (the manuscript's reference [21]). Nothing in its Sections 10 to 12 uses Theorem 1.2. The proof of Lemma 9.2 was read: a contour shift, the functional equation, and a uniform zero count; it is standard. The statement of Theorem 1.2 was read, but its proof, which runs through the manuscript's cubic-theta and sextic-large-sieve machinery over every cyclotomic field containing $`\zeta_{12}`$, was not independently verified.

**Plausibility consideration.** Hypothesis B with $`(M,c,u)=(8,4,5)`$ implies that at least a constant times $`x/(\log x)^2`$ primes $`p\le2x`$ have a prime factor of $`p-1`$ exceeding $`x^{0.9}`$. The published record for a positive proportion of primes is exponent $`0.677`$ (Baker–Harman, [S14](references.md#s14)), and a 2025 preprint reaches $`0.679`$ for infinitely many primes. Family 011 claims the full Poisson–Dirichlet law for the prime factors of $`p-1`$, resolving the Ford–Konyagin–Luca conjecture. No theorem forbids these statements, and this record does not assert that they are false; it records that the premises are far beyond the published literature, so that a reader can weigh them.

### What those numbers mean

The counts are far larger than $`x/(\log x)^2`$ because the true density of such primes is positive; the theorem claims only the weaker lower bound that the method yields. The agreement of the two criteria on every tested prime, and the independence from the choice of prime ideal above $`p`$, are the checks of Lemmas 5 and 7. The agreement with Shanks's density is a classical observation and plays no role in the proof. None of this bears on Hypotheses A, B or C.
