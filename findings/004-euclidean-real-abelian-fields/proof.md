# Euclidean rings of integers in real abelian fields from uniform primitive roots

**Roger Malcolm III · Research and drafting assistance using Fable 5.1**

**Version 0.1.0 · 8 October 2026**

[Overview](README.md) · [Verification record](verification.md) · [References](references.md)

**Status: conditional theorem, complete proof draft, pending independent specialist review.** The theorems below assume three statements of OpenAI's manuscript family 029, formulated as Hypotheses A, B and C in Section 1; one of them, Hypothesis B, is itself proved in that manuscript by importing a theorem of family 011. Neither family is formalized in the repository's Lean catalogue. The present work does not independently certify those analytic inputs. The downstream argument has received AI-assisted derivation and adversarial checks, but not formal verification or independent specialist review. Publication of this draft is not a correctness or priority certificate.

**Abstract.** Assuming a uniform zero-free strip for finite-order Hecke $`L`$-functions of cyclotomic fields, a prime-construction proposition supplying $`\gg x/(\log x)^2`$ primes $`p\equiv u\pmod M`$ whose predecessor $`p-1`$ has a very large prime factor and a controlled cofactor, and a uniform smooth explicit formula for number fields with such a strip, we prove that for every real abelian number field $`K`$ and every primitive non-torsion unit $`\varepsilon`$ of $`K`$, at least $`c\,x/(\log x)^2`$ prime ideals $`\mathfrak p`$ of norm at most $`x`$ have residue field generated by $`-1`$ and $`\varepsilon`$. By Graves's growth criterion, every real abelian field of class number one is then Euclidean; in particular every real quadratic principal ideal domain is Euclidean. The same count gives infinitely many primes with a Fibonacci primitive root, with a lower bound of order $`x/(\log x)^2`$. The argument is the manuscript's own proof for integer bases, transplanted to units, with two new ingredients: the Kummer field of a unit has discriminant controlled by $`q^q`$ alone, and the residue class $`p\equiv-1\pmod f`$, $`p\equiv3\pmod4`$ forces complete splitting in a real field while making the unit $`-1`$ a quadratic non-residue.

Weinberger proved the Euclidean conclusion in 1973 under the generalized Riemann hypothesis, and Harper and Murty proved it unconditionally for unit rank at least four. The argument developed here concerns unit rank one to three; it does not claim either of those results as a new discovery. [S4](references.md#s4), [S6](references.md#s6)

## 1. Premises and notation

Throughout, $`K`$ is a real abelian number field of degree $`n=[K:\mathbb Q]\ge2`$, with conductor $`f`$, ring of integers $`\mathcal O_K`$ and unit group $`\mathcal O_K^{\times}`$. By the Kronecker–Weber theorem $`K\subset\mathbb Q(\zeta_f)`$, and a rational prime $`p\nmid f`$ splits completely in $`K`$ exactly when its residue class lies in the subgroup

```math
H_K=\ker\bigl((\mathbb Z/f\mathbb Z)^{\times}\to\operatorname{Gal}(K/\mathbb Q)\bigr).
```

Since $`K`$ is real, complex conjugation acts trivially on it, so $`-1\in H_K`$.

A unit $`\varepsilon\in\mathcal O_K^{\times}`$ is called **primitive** if it is not a root of unity and, for every prime $`q`$, it is not of the form $`\pm\eta^q`$ with $`\eta\in\mathcal O_K^{\times}`$. Any element of a basis of the free abelian group $`\mathcal O_K^{\times}/\{\pm1\}`$ is primitive, so primitive units exist as soon as $`n\ge2`$. For a primitive unit put

```math
h(\varepsilon)=\max_{\sigma}\,|\sigma(\varepsilon)|>1,
```

the maximum over the $`n`$ real embeddings. For an odd prime $`q`$ let

```math
K_q=K\bigl(\zeta_q,\varepsilon^{1/q}\bigr),
```

the splitting field over $`K`$ of $`(T^q-\varepsilon)(T^q-1)`$; it does not depend on the choice of $`q`$-th root. We write $`L=\log x`$, and $`N\mathfrak p`$ for the absolute norm of a prime ideal.

### 1.1 The three source premises

The following three statements are taken from the manuscript *Primitive roots for every admissible integer base* (family 029 of the OpenAI mathematics collection, pinned in [S1](references.md#s1)). They are used as black boxes. Nothing else from that manuscript is used; in particular its headline theorem on integer bases is not used.

**Hypothesis A — uniform zero-free strip (manuscript Theorem 1.2).** Let $`F`$ be a cyclotomic field containing $`\zeta_{12}`$ and $`\chi`$ a finite-order Hecke character of $`F`$. Then the meromorphic continuation of $`L_F(s,\chi)`$ has no zeros in the half-plane $`\Re s>1-\theta_0`$, where $`\theta_0=10^{-6}`$, the pole of the principal character at $`s=1`$ excepted. The width $`\theta_0`$ is the same for every $`F`$ and $`\chi`$. Only the existence of a uniform width is used below; no constant from the source enters.

**Hypothesis B — prime construction (manuscript Proposition 2.1).** Let $`M`$ be a fixed positive multiple of $`8`$, $`c\in\{2,4\}`$, and $`u`$ an integer with $`\gcd(u,M)=1`$, $`c\mid u-1`$ and $`\gcd\bigl((u-1)/c,\,M/c\bigr)=1`$. For every sufficiently large $`x`$ there are at least $`c_{M,c,u}\,x/L^2`$ distinct primes $`p\in(x,2x)`$ with

```math
p\equiv u\pmod M,\qquad p-1=c\,r\,Q,
```

where $`Q>x^{0.9}`$ is prime and every prime factor of $`r`$ lies in $`[\exp(L^{0.1}),\exp(L^{0.3})]`$. The statement contains no base: the integer $`a`$ of the manuscript's headline theorem does not occur in it. Its proof in the manuscript's Section 12 uses the Bombieri–Vinogradov theorem, the prime number theorem in progressions, two elementary sieve lemmas, and a bilinear "marked Type II" estimate imported from Theorem 3.1 of family 011, *The Poisson–Dirichlet law for prime predecessors*. Hypothesis B therefore depends on two unformalized manuscripts.

**Hypothesis C — uniform smooth explicit formula (manuscript Lemma 9.2).** Fix $`0<\theta<1/2`$ and a nonnegative $`f_0\in C_c^{\infty}((0,\infty))`$. Let $`K'`$ be any number field, of degree $`n'`$ and absolute discriminant $`D'`$, whose Dedekind zeta function has no zero in $`\Re s>1-\theta`$. Then for every real $`x\ge2`$,

```math
\psi_{K',f_0}(x)=\sum_{\mathfrak P}\ \sum_{j\ge1}(\log N\mathfrak P)\,f_0\!\left(\frac{(N\mathfrak P)^j}{x}\right)
\ \ll_{f_0}\ x+x^{1-\theta}\bigl(\log D'+n'\bigr),
```

with an implied constant independent of $`K'`$ and $`x`$.

### 1.2 The external criterion

**Theorem G (Graves, [S2](references.md#s2), Theorem 2 and Corollary 1).** Let $`K`$ be a number field with infinitely many units and $`C`$ an ideal of $`\mathcal O_K`$ whose class generates the class group. If, for all sufficiently large $`x`$,

```math
\#\bigl\{\mathfrak p\ \text{prime}:\ [\mathfrak p]=[C],\ N\mathfrak p\le x,\ \mathcal O_K^{\times}\twoheadrightarrow(\mathcal O_K/\mathfrak p)^{\times}\bigr\}\ \gg\ \frac{x}{(\log x)^2},
```

then $`C`$ is a Euclidean ideal in Lenstra's sense. For $`C=\mathcal O_K`$ and class number one the conclusion is that $`\mathcal O_K`$ is a Euclidean ideal of itself, which is the statement that $`\mathcal O_K`$ is a Euclidean domain: with Graves's Definition 1 applied to $`C=R`$, the Euclidean function $`\phi(b)=\psi(b^{-1}R)`$ satisfies division with smaller remainder.

Standard algebraic number theory is used without comment and is collected in [S13](references.md#s13).

## 2. The main conditional theorems

**Theorem 1 (the count).** Assume Hypotheses A, B and C. Let $`K`$ be a real abelian number field of degree at least two, of any class number, and let $`\varepsilon`$ be a primitive unit of $`K`$. Then for all sufficiently large $`x`$,

```math
\#\bigl\{\mathfrak p\ \text{prime ideal of }\mathcal O_K:\ N\mathfrak p\le x,\ \langle-1,\varepsilon\rangle\twoheadrightarrow(\mathcal O_K/\mathfrak p)^{\times}\bigr\}
\ \ge\ c_{K,\varepsilon}\,\frac{x}{(\log x)^2}
```

with some $`c_{K,\varepsilon}>0`$. This is the $`x/(\log x)^2`$ form of Artin's conjecture for the unit $`\varepsilon`$, with the unit $`-1`$ allowed as a second generator.

**Theorem 2 (Euclidean algorithm).** Assume Hypotheses A, B and C. Every real abelian number field of degree at least two and class number one has a Euclidean ring of integers. In particular every real quadratic field of class number one is Euclidean.

*Proof of Theorem 2 from Theorem 1.* The unit group of $`K`$ is infinite because $`n\ge2`$ and $`K`$ is real. Class number one means that the class of $`\mathcal O_K`$ generates the class group. Surjectivity of $`\langle-1,\varepsilon\rangle\to(\mathcal O_K/\mathfrak p)^{\times}`$ implies surjectivity of $`\mathcal O_K^{\times}`$, so Theorem 1 supplies the hypothesis of Theorem G with $`C=\mathcal O_K`$. $`\blacksquare`$

**Corollary 3 (Fibonacci primitive roots).** Assume Hypotheses A, B and C. At least $`c\,x/(\log x)^2`$ primes $`p\le x`$ possess a primitive root $`g`$ with $`g^2\equiv g+1\pmod p`$. In particular infinitely many primes have a Fibonacci primitive root. More generally, for every real quadratic field with a unit $`\varepsilon`$ of norm $`-1`$ and minimal polynomial $`T^2-tT-1`$, at least $`c\,x/(\log x)^2`$ primes $`p\le x`$ have a primitive root which is a root of $`T^2-tT-1`$.

The proof of Theorem 1 occupies Sections 3 to 7; Section 8 records the passage to Theorem 2, and Section 9 proves Corollary 3.

## 3. Kummer fields of a unit

**Lemma 1.** Let $`q`$ be an odd prime with $`q\nmid f`$. Then $`\varepsilon`$ is not a $`q`$-th power in $`K(\zeta_q)`$, and $`[K_q:K(\zeta_q)]=q`$.

*Proof.* Since $`q\nmid f`$, the fields $`K`$ and $`\mathbb Q(\zeta_q)`$ have coprime conductors, so $`K\cap\mathbb Q(\zeta_q)=\mathbb Q`$ and $`m=[K(\zeta_q):K]=q-1`$, which is coprime to $`q`$. Suppose $`\varepsilon=\alpha^q`$ with $`\alpha\in K(\zeta_q)`$. Taking the norm to $`K`$ gives $`\varepsilon^m=\beta^q`$ with $`\beta=N_{K(\zeta_q)/K}(\alpha)\in K`$; $`\beta`$ is a unit because $`\varepsilon`$ is. Writing $`1=am+bq`$ with integers $`a,b`$,

```math
\varepsilon=(\varepsilon^m)^a(\varepsilon^b)^q=(\beta^a\varepsilon^b)^q,
```

so $`\varepsilon`$ is a $`q`$-th power of a unit of $`K`$, contradicting primitivity. Since $`\zeta_q\in K(\zeta_q)`$, the extension $`K_q/K(\zeta_q)`$ is a Kummer extension of degree dividing $`q`$, and it is not trivial. $`\blacksquare`$

**Lemma 2.** Let $`q`$ be an odd prime with $`q>f`$. Then $`n_q=[K_q:\mathbb Q]=n\,q\,(q-1)`$, every rational prime $`p\nmid qf`$ is unramified in $`K_q`$, and

```math
\log D_{K_q}+n_q\ \le\ n_q\bigl(\log|d_K|+3\log q\bigr)\ \ll_K\ n_q\log(2q),
```

where $`D_{K_q}`$ is the absolute discriminant of $`K_q`$ and $`d_K`$ that of $`K`$.

*Proof.* The degree is $`n(q-1)`$ for $`K(\zeta_q)`$ by coprimality of conductors, times $`q`$ by Lemma 1. Because $`d_K\mid f^{\,n}`$ and $`\gcd(f,q)=1`$, the discriminants of $`K`$ and $`\mathbb Q(\zeta_q)`$ are coprime, so

```math
|d_{K(\zeta_q)}|=|d_K|^{\,q-1}\,\bigl(q^{\,q-2}\bigr)^{n}.
```

The element $`\varepsilon^{1/q}`$ is an algebraic integer with minimal polynomial $`T^q-\varepsilon`$ over $`K(\zeta_q)`$ (Lemma 1), whose discriminant is $`\pm q^q\varepsilon^{q-1}`$, a unit times $`q^q`$. Hence the relative discriminant of $`K_q/K(\zeta_q)`$ divides $`(q^q)`$, and the tower formula gives

```math
D_{K_q}=|d_{K(\zeta_q)}|^{\,q}\,N\bigl(\mathfrak d_{K_q/K(\zeta_q)}\bigr)
\ \le\ \bigl(|d_K|^{\,q-1}q^{\,n(q-2)}\bigr)^{q}\,q^{\,qn(q-1)}.
```

Taking logarithms,

```math
\log D_{K_q}\le nq(q-1)\log|d_K|+nq(q-2)\log q+nq(q-1)\log q\le n_q\bigl(\log|d_K|+2\log q\bigr),
```

and adding $`n_q\le n_q\log q`$ for $`q\ge3`$ gives the display. For the ramification statement: $`K(\zeta_q)/\mathbb Q`$ is unramified outside $`fq`$, and $`K_q/K(\zeta_q)`$ is unramified outside $`q`$ because its relative discriminant divides $`(q^q)`$. $`\blacksquare`$

The point of Lemma 2 is that the unit contributes nothing to the discriminant. For an integer base $`a`$ the corresponding bound in the source carries a factor $`|a|^{q-1}`$; the shape $`\log D+n\ll n\log(2q)`$ needed in Hypothesis C is the same.

## 4. Zero-free regions and the uniform splitting bound

**Lemma 3.** Assume Hypothesis A. For every odd prime $`q`$, the Dedekind zeta function $`\zeta_{K_q}(s)`$ has no zero with $`\Re s>1-\theta_0`$.

*Proof.* Let $`F_q=\mathbb Q(\zeta_{12qf})`$, a cyclotomic field containing $`\zeta_{12}`$, $`\zeta_q`$ and, by Kronecker–Weber, $`K`$. The compositum $`K_qF_q=F_q(\varepsilon^{1/q})`$ is a Kummer extension of $`F_q`$, hence cyclic of degree dividing $`q`$. The factorization of the Dedekind zeta function of an abelian extension gives

```math
\zeta_{K_qF_q}(s)=\prod_{\chi}L_{F_q}(s,\chi),
```

the product over the characters of $`\operatorname{Gal}(K_qF_q/F_q)`$. Each factor is a finite-order Hecke $`L`$-function of the cyclotomic field $`F_q\ni\zeta_{12}`$, hence zero-free in $`\Re s>1-\theta_0`$ by Hypothesis A, so $`\zeta_{K_qF_q}`$ is zero-free there. Next, $`K_qF_q/K_q`$ is Galois with group isomorphic to $`\operatorname{Gal}\bigl(F_q/(F_q\cap K_q)\bigr)`$, a subgroup of the abelian group $`\operatorname{Gal}(F_q/\mathbb Q)`$, so

```math
\zeta_{K_qF_q}(s)=\zeta_{K_q}(s)\prod_{\psi\ne1}L_{K_q}(s,\psi)
```

with entire non-principal Hecke $`L`$-functions $`L_{K_q}(s,\psi)`$. A zero of $`\zeta_{K_q}`$ in the half-plane would be a zero of the left side. $`\blacksquare`$

This is the source's argument for the fields $`\mathbb Q(\zeta_q,a^{1/q})`$, with the auxiliary cyclotomic field enlarged by the factor $`f`$ so that it contains $`K`$.

**Lemma 4 (uniform splitting bound).** Assume Hypotheses A and C. Fix $`f_0\in C_c^{\infty}((0,\infty))`$ with $`f_0\ge0`$ and $`f_0=1`$ on $`[1,2]`$. For all sufficiently large $`x`$ and every odd prime $`q`$ with $`f<q\le\exp(L^{0.3})`$,

```math
S_q(x)=\#\bigl\{\mathfrak p\ \text{prime of }\mathcal O_K:\ N\mathfrak p\in(x,2x],\ \mathfrak p\ \text{splits completely in }K_q\bigr\}
\ \ll_K\ \frac{x}{q(q-1)L}+x^{1-\theta_0},
```

with implied constant and threshold independent of $`q`$. Here "splits completely" means that every prime of $`K_q`$ above $`\mathfrak p`$ has residue degree and ramification index one over $`\mathfrak p`$; since $`K_q/K`$ is Galois, it suffices that one prime above $`\mathfrak p`$ does.

*Proof.* Apply Hypothesis C to $`K'=K_q`$ with $`\theta=\theta_0`$, which is allowed by Lemma 3. Each $`\mathfrak p`$ counted by $`S_q(x)`$ lies under exactly $`[K_q:K]=q(q-1)`$ primes $`\mathfrak P`$ of $`K_q`$, each with $`N\mathfrak P=N\mathfrak p\in(x,2x]`$, and each contributes $`(\log N\mathfrak P)f_0(N\mathfrak P/x)\ge L`$ to the nonnegative sum $`\psi_{K_q,f_0}(x)`$ through its $`j=1`$ term. Hence, by Lemma 2,

```math
q(q-1)\,L\,S_q(x)\ \le\ \psi_{K_q,f_0}(x)\ \ll_{f_0}\ x+x^{1-\theta_0}\bigl(\log D_{K_q}+n_q\bigr)\ \ll_K\ x+x^{1-\theta_0}\,n_q\log(2q).
```

Divide by $`q(q-1)L`$, use $`n_q=nq(q-1)`$, and note $`\log(2q)\le L`$ for $`q\le\exp(L^{0.3})`$ and $`x`$ large. $`\blacksquare`$

Counting prime ideals of $`K`$ rather than rational primes is what makes Lemma 4 work for $`n>2`$, where $`K_q/\mathbb Q`$ need not be Galois.

## 5. The splitting test

**Lemma 5.** Let $`q`$ be an odd prime with $`q>f`$, let $`p\nmid qf`$ be a rational prime, and let $`\mathfrak p`$ be a prime of $`\mathcal O_K`$ above $`p`$. Then $`\mathfrak p`$ splits completely in $`K_q`$ if and only if

1. $`p`$ splits completely in $`K`$, that is, $`p\bmod f\in H_K`$;
2. $`p\equiv1\pmod q`$;
3. $`\varepsilon^{(p-1)/q}\equiv1\pmod{\mathfrak p}`$.

*Proof.* The prime $`\mathfrak p`$ splits completely in $`K_q`$ if and only if it splits completely in $`K(\zeta_q)`$ and some, equivalently every, prime $`\mathfrak Q`$ of $`K(\zeta_q)`$ above $`\mathfrak p`$ splits completely in $`K_q=K(\zeta_q)(\varepsilon^{1/q})`$. Complete splitting in the abelian compositum $`K(\zeta_q)=K\cdot\mathbb Q(\zeta_q)`$ means that the residue field of $`\mathfrak p`$ is $`\mathbb F_p`$ and that $`p\equiv1\pmod q`$, which is conditions 1 and 2. Given these, the residue field at $`\mathfrak Q`$ is $`\mathbb F_p`$ and contains the $`q`$-th roots of unity. Since $`p`$ is unramified in $`K_q`$ by Lemma 2, $`\mathfrak Q`$ splits completely in the Kummer extension if and only if $`T^q-\varepsilon`$ splits into distinct linear factors over $`\mathbb F_p`$, that is, if and only if the residue of $`\varepsilon`$ lies in $`(\mathbb F_p^{\times})^q`$, that is, $`\varepsilon^{(p-1)/q}\equiv1\pmod{\mathfrak Q}`$. As $`\mathfrak Q\cap\mathcal O_K=\mathfrak p`$ and $`\mathcal O_K/\mathfrak p=\mathbb F_p`$, this is condition 3. $`\blacksquare`$

## 6. The progression and the unit minus one

**Lemma 6.** Let $`M=\operatorname{lcm}(8,f)`$. There is an integer $`u`$ with $`\gcd(u,M)=1`$, $`u\equiv3\pmod4`$ and $`u\equiv-1\pmod f`$ such that $`c=2`$ and $`u`$ satisfy the hypotheses of Hypothesis B, namely $`2\mid u-1`$ and $`\gcd\bigl((u-1)/2,\,M/2\bigr)=1`$. Every prime $`p\equiv u\pmod M`$ satisfies $`p\equiv3\pmod4`$, $`p\nmid f`$, and $`p`$ splits completely in $`K`$.

*Proof.* Choose $`u\equiv-1\pmod f`$ together with $`u\equiv3`$ or $`7\pmod8`$; the two congruences are compatible by the Chinese remainder theorem (if $`8\mid f`$ then $`u\equiv-1\equiv7\pmod8`$ already; if $`4\mid f`$ but $`8\nmid f`$ then $`u\equiv-1\equiv3\pmod4`$ and either lift works; if $`f`$ is odd, take $`u\equiv3\pmod8`$). Then $`u\equiv3\pmod4`$, so $`(u-1)/2`$ is odd. Every odd prime $`\ell\mid M`$ divides $`f`$, and $`u\equiv-1\not\equiv1\pmod\ell`$, so $`\ell\nmid(u-1)/2`$. Hence $`\gcd\bigl((u-1)/2,M/2\bigr)=1`$ and $`\gcd(u,M)=1`$.

If $`p\equiv u\pmod M`$ then $`p\equiv-1\pmod f`$. The Frobenius of $`p`$ in $`\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb Q(\zeta_f)/\mathbb Q)\cong(\mathbb Z/f\mathbb Z)^{\times}`$ is the class of $`-1`$, which is complex conjugation. Complex conjugation is trivial on the real field $`K`$, so $`p`$ splits completely in $`K`$; equivalently $`-1\in H_K`$. $`\blacksquare`$

For $`K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)`$ one may take $`M=40`$ and $`u=19`$; for $`K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt2)`$, $`M=8`$ and $`u=7`$. This is the only place where the hypothesis that $`K`$ is real is used.

**Lemma 7.** Let $`p\equiv3\pmod4`$ be a prime that splits completely in $`K`$, let $`\mathfrak p\mid p`$, so that $`(\mathcal O_K/\mathfrak p)^{\times}=\mathbb F_p^{\times}`$ is cyclic of order $`p-1`$, and let $`H_{\mathfrak p}`$ be the image of $`\langle-1,\varepsilon\rangle`$ in $`\mathbb F_p^{\times}`$, of index $`i_{\mathfrak p}`$.

1. $`i_{\mathfrak p}`$ is odd.
2. If $`q`$ is a prime dividing $`i_{\mathfrak p}`$, then $`q`$ is odd and $`\varepsilon^{(p-1)/q}\equiv1\pmod{\mathfrak p}`$.

*Proof.* (1) The element $`-1`$ lies in $`H_{\mathfrak p}`$ and is a non-square modulo $`p`$ because $`p\equiv3\pmod4`$. If $`i_{\mathfrak p}`$ were even, $`H_{\mathfrak p}`$ would lie in the unique subgroup of index two, the squares. (2) If $`q\mid i_{\mathfrak p}`$ then $`H_{\mathfrak p}`$ lies in the unique subgroup of index $`q`$, which is $`(\mathbb F_p^{\times})^q`$; in particular $`\varepsilon`$ is a $`q`$-th power modulo $`\mathfrak p`$, which is the stated congruence. $`\blacksquare`$

Lemma 7 is the device that handles the prime $`2`$. In the source, the prime $`2`$ is handled by placing the integer base $`a`$ in a progression of quadratic non-residues through quadratic reciprocity; a unit has no such progression, but the unit $`-1`$ is available and is a non-residue exactly when $`p\equiv3\pmod4`$.

## 7. Proof of the counting theorem

Fix $`K`$ and a primitive unit $`\varepsilon`$, and take $`(M,c,u)=(\operatorname{lcm}(8,f),2,u)`$ from Lemma 6. For sufficiently large $`x`$, let $`S(x)`$ be the set of primes supplied by Hypothesis B: $`|S(x)|\gg_K x/L^2`$, every $`p\in S(x)`$ lies in $`(x,2x)`$, satisfies $`p\equiv u\pmod M`$, and has $`p-1=2rQ`$ with $`Q>x^{0.9}`$ prime and all prime factors of $`r`$ in $`[\exp(L^{0.1}),\exp(L^{0.3})]`$. By Lemma 6 each such $`p`$ is $`\equiv3\pmod4`$ and splits completely in $`K`$, so $`(\mathcal O_K/\mathfrak p)^{\times}=\mathbb F_p^{\times}`$ for each of the $`n`$ primes $`\mathfrak p\mid p`$.

Call $`p\in S(x)`$ **bad** if $`i_{\mathfrak p}>1`$ for some $`\mathfrak p\mid p`$. For a bad $`p`$ choose such a $`\mathfrak p`$ and a prime $`q\mid i_{\mathfrak p}`$. By Lemma 7, $`q`$ is odd and $`\varepsilon^{(p-1)/q}\equiv1\pmod{\mathfrak p}`$; since $`p-1=2rQ`$ and $`q`$ is odd, $`q\mid rQ`$. Two cases.

**Case $`q=Q`$.** Put $`j=(p-1)/Q=2r<2x/x^{0.9}=2x^{0.1}`$. The order of $`\varepsilon`$ modulo $`\mathfrak p`$ divides $`j`$, so $`\mathfrak p\mid(\varepsilon^{j}-1)`$ and $`p`$ divides the rational integer

```math
b_j=N_{K/\mathbb Q}\bigl(\varepsilon^{j}-1\bigr)=\prod_{\sigma}\bigl(\sigma(\varepsilon)^{j}-1\bigr).
```

Each factor is nonzero because $`\varepsilon`$ is not a root of unity, and has absolute value at most $`h(\varepsilon)^{j}+1\le2h(\varepsilon)^{j}`$, so $`0<|b_j|\le2^n h(\varepsilon)^{nj}`$. Thus $`p`$ divides

```math
B(x)=\prod_{1\le j<2x^{0.1}}|b_j|,\qquad
\log B(x)\le\sum_{j<2x^{0.1}}n\bigl(j\log h(\varepsilon)+\log2\bigr)\ \ll_{K,\varepsilon}\ x^{0.2}.
```

A prime $`p>x`$ dividing $`B(x)`$ contributes at least $`L`$ to $`\log B(x)`$, so the number of bad primes of this type is $`O_{K,\varepsilon}(x^{0.2}/L)=o(x/L^2)`$.

**Case $`q\mid r`$.** Then $`\exp(L^{0.1})\le q\le\exp(L^{0.3})`$, so $`q>f`$ for large $`x`$, and $`p\nmid qf`$. Conditions 1, 2 and 3 of Lemma 5 hold by Lemma 6, by $`q\mid p-1`$, and by Lemma 7(2) respectively. Hence $`\mathfrak p`$ splits completely in $`K_q`$ and is counted by $`S_q(x)`$. By Lemma 4 the number of bad primes of this type is at most

```math
\sum_{\exp(L^{0.1})\le q\le\exp(L^{0.3})}S_q(x)
\ \ll_K\ \frac{x}{L}\sum_{m>\exp(L^{0.1})}\frac{1}{m(m-1)}+x^{1-\theta_0}\exp(L^{0.3})
\ \ll_K\ \frac{x\,e^{-L^{0.1}}}{L}+x\,e^{-\theta_0L+L^{0.3}}
\ =\ o\!\left(\frac{x}{L^2}\right),
```

the first sum telescoping to $`\exp(-L^{0.1})`$. This is the source's estimate (2.6) verbatim.

Hence for all large $`x`$ at least $`|S(x)|-o(x/L^2)\gg_{K,\varepsilon}x/L^2`$ primes $`p\in(x,2x)`$ have $`i_{\mathfrak p}=1`$ for every $`\mathfrak p\mid p`$: the units $`-1`$ and $`\varepsilon`$ generate $`(\mathcal O_K/\mathfrak p)^{\times}`$ for each of the $`n`$ prime ideals above $`p`$. Each such $`\mathfrak p`$ has $`N\mathfrak p=p\le2x`$. Replacing $`x`$ by $`x/2`$ gives Theorem 1. $`\blacksquare`$

<a id="euclidean"></a>

## 8. From the count to a Euclidean algorithm

Theorem 2 follows from Theorem 1 and Theorem G as shown in Section 2. Two remarks on what each hypothesis does.

Class number one is used only in the final step, to ensure that the class of $`\mathcal O_K`$ generates the class group. For a real abelian field of class number greater than one, Theorem 1 still holds and asserts nothing false; it gives the primitive-root count without the Euclidean conclusion. Extending Theorem 2 to Euclidean ideal classes in the sense of Lenstra would require primes in a prescribed ideal class, which is a Chebotarev condition in the Hilbert class field rather than a congruence condition, and Hypothesis B as stated does not supply it. This is not attempted here.

The hypothesis that $`K`$ is real is used only in Lemma 6, in two ways at once: complete splitting of $`p\equiv-1\pmod f`$ needs complex conjugation to be trivial on $`K`$, and the parity device of Lemma 7 needs $`p\equiv3\pmod4`$ together with the unit $`-1`$. For an imaginary abelian field the residue class $`-1`$ does not split, and the argument stops at Lemma 6. The scope section records what would be needed there.

## 9. Fibonacci primitive roots

*Proof of Corollary 3.* Take $`K=\mathbb Q(\sqrt5)`$ and $`\varepsilon=\varphi=(1+\sqrt5)/2`$, a primitive unit of norm $`-1`$ with conjugate $`\varphi'=-1/\varphi`$. Run the proof of Theorem 1. It produces, for all large $`x`$, at least $`c\,x/L^2`$ primes $`p\in(x,2x)`$ with $`p\equiv3\pmod4`$, $`p`$ split in $`K`$, and $`\langle-1,\varphi\rangle=\mathbb F_p^{\times}`$ modulo a prime $`\mathfrak p\mid p`$. Since $`p`$ splits, $`T^2-T-1`$ has two roots in $`\mathbb F_p`$, namely $`r_1=\varphi\bmod\mathfrak p`$ and $`r_2=\varphi'\bmod\mathfrak p`$, with $`r_1r_2=\varphi\varphi'=-1`$.

Write $`p-1=2m`$ with $`m`$ odd. Because $`\langle-1,r_1\rangle=\mathbb F_p^{\times}`$, either $`r_1`$ has order $`2m`$ and is a primitive root, or $`r_1`$ has order $`m`$ and $`-1\notin\langle r_1\rangle`$, in which case $`\langle r_1\rangle`$ is the subgroup of squares. In the second case $`r_2=-r_1^{-1}`$ satisfies

```math
r_2^{\,m}=(-1)^m\bigl(r_1^{-1}\bigr)^{m}=-1,
```

because $`m`$ is odd, so $`r_2`$ has order $`2m`$ and is a primitive root. In either case some root of $`T^2-T-1`$ is a primitive root modulo $`p`$. The same computation applies to any unit of norm $`-1`$ in a real quadratic field, since then $`\varepsilon'=-1/\varepsilon`$. $`\blacksquare`$

In fact exactly one of the two roots is a primitive root when $`p\equiv3\pmod4`$: if $`r_1`$ is primitive then $`r_2=r_1^{(p-3)/2}`$ has order $`(p-1)/2`$. For a unit of norm $`+1`$ the same argument gives only that one of $`\varepsilon`$, $`-\varepsilon`$ is a primitive root; whether $`\varepsilon`$ itself is one is a Chebotarev condition in a non-abelian extension and is not controlled by the congruence machinery of Hypothesis B.

## 10. Where the argument departs from the source

The source proves Theorem 1 of family 029 for an integer base $`a`$. The present argument is that proof with the following five local replacements; everything else, in particular Hypotheses A, B and C, is used as stated.

1. **Not a $`q`$-th power.** The source's Lemma 9.1 uses a prime divisor of the integer $`a`$ and valuations to show that $`a`$ is not a $`q`$-th power in $`\mathbb Q(\zeta_q)`$. A unit has no prime divisor; Lemma 1 replaces this by a norm argument using $`\gcd(q-1,q)=1`$ and the primitivity of $`\varepsilon`$.
2. **Discriminant.** The source bounds the discriminant of $`\mathbb Q(\zeta_q,a^{1/q})`$ by $`q^{q(q-2)}(q^q|a|^{q-1})^{q-1}`$. Lemma 2 uses the compositum formula with $`K`$ and the fact that $`T^q-\varepsilon`$ has discriminant a unit times $`q^q`$. The shape $`\log D+n\ll n\log(2q)`$ required by Hypothesis C is unchanged.
3. **Auxiliary cyclotomic field.** Lemma 3 enlarges the source's $`\mathbb Q(\zeta_{12q})`$ to $`\mathbb Q(\zeta_{12qf})`$ so that it contains $`K`$.
4. **The progression.** The source's Lemma 2.4 places $`a`$ in a progression of quadratic non-residues by quadratic reciprocity. Lemma 6 instead uses the class $`u\equiv-1\pmod f`$, $`u\equiv3\pmod4`$, which gives complete splitting in the real field and makes the unit $`-1`$ a non-residue (Lemma 7). This is the new ingredient, and the place where "real" is needed.
5. **Prime ideals.** The assembly in Section 7 replaces $`a^{j}-1`$ by the norm $`N(\varepsilon^{j}-1)`$ and counts prime ideals $`\mathfrak p`$ of $`K`$ in place of rational primes. Lemma 4 is stated for prime ideals of $`K`$, which avoids any need for $`K_q/\mathbb Q`$ to be Galois.

## 11. Scope, attribution, and dependencies

**What is proved.** Theorems 1 and 2 and Corollary 3 are proved from Hypotheses A, B and C and Theorem G, with every other step written out above. The elementary steps (Lemmas 1, 2, 5, 6, 7, the assembly, and the Fibonacci computation) were checked by hand and, for the mechanisms accessible to computation, on every prime below one million; see the [verification record](verification.md).

**Dependencies.** Hypotheses A and C are Theorem 1.2 and Lemma 9.2 of the family-029 manuscript. Hypothesis B is its Proposition 2.1, whose proof imports Theorem 3.1 of the family-011 manuscript *The Poisson–Dirichlet law for prime predecessors*. Neither manuscript appears in the repository's Lean formalization catalogue at the pinned commit. The present theorems therefore inherit the status of two unformalized claimed results. Hypothesis B alone implies that $`\gg x/(\log x)^2`$ primes $`p\le2x`$ have $`p-1`$ divisible by a prime exceeding $`x^{0.9}`$, which is far beyond what is established in the published literature; this is recorded as a plausibility consideration in the verification record, not as a contradiction, since no theorem forbids it.

**What is not claimed.** No novelty is claimed for Theorem G, for the three-range structure of the argument, which is Hooley's [S9](references.md#s9), or for the large-prime-factor device, which goes back to Gupta–Murty and Heath-Brown [S10](references.md#s10). Weinberger's GRH-conditional theorem [S6](references.md#s6), the Harper–Murty theorem for unit rank at least four [S4](references.md#s4), and Narkiewicz's theorem that at most two real quadratic fields of class number one are non-Euclidean [S7](references.md#s7) are prior results; the proposed advance is the removal of the unit-rank restriction and of the possible exceptions, conditional on the premises. Imaginary abelian fields with infinitely many units, Euclidean ideal classes, and the question whether a unit of norm $`+1`$ is itself a primitive root for many primes are outside the statements proved here. A possible extension to imaginary abelian fields would need a version of Hypothesis B with $`c=|\mu_K|`$ and a residue class avoiding $`1`$ modulo every prime power dividing $`|\mu_K|`$ one step further; it is left as a direction, not asserted.
