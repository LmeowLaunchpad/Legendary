## The conditional theorem

Assume the manuscript's uniform zero-free strip for finite-order Hecke $`L`$-functions of cyclotomic fields, its prime-construction proposition, and its uniform smooth explicit formula.

Then **every real abelian number field of class number one has a Euclidean ring of integers.** In particular every real quadratic field of class number one is Euclidean, which Weinberger proved in 1973 under the generalized Riemann hypothesis.

The Euclidean conclusion comes from a counting theorem that holds for every real abelian field $`K`$ regardless of class number. Call a unit $`\varepsilon`$ of $`K`$ primitive if it is not a root of unity and not of the form $`\pm\eta^q`$ for a prime $`q`$ and a unit $`\eta`$. Then for every primitive unit and all sufficiently large $`x`$,

```math
\#\bigl\{\mathfrak p:\ N\mathfrak p\le x,\ \langle-1,\varepsilon\rangle\twoheadrightarrow(\mathcal O_K/\mathfrak p)^{\times}\bigr\}
\ \ge\ c_{K,\varepsilon}\,\frac{x}{(\log x)^2}.
```

This is the $`x/(\log x)^2`$ form of Artin's conjecture for the unit $`\varepsilon`$, with $`-1`$ allowed as a second generator. Graves's growth criterion turns such a count, when the class number is one, into a Euclidean algorithm. [Main theorems and proof](proof.md#main-theorem).

## In plain language

A ring of integers is Euclidean when it has a division-with-remainder algorithm for some measure of size. For the ordinary integers the size is the absolute value. For a finite list of quadratic rings the absolute value of the norm works, and that list has been known to be complete for decades. For every other real quadratic ring with class number one, the norm fails as a size function, and the question was whether some other size function works. Weinberger showed in 1973 that it always does if the generalized Riemann hypothesis holds. Without that hypothesis the question has stayed open for the small unit ranks, even though Narkiewicz showed in 2007 that there can be at most two exceptional real quadratic fields.

Harper, Murty and Graves showed that a Euclidean algorithm follows once enough prime ideals have residue fields generated by the units of the ring. The present note observes that the OpenAI manuscript on Artin's conjecture contains an engine that produces such primes in quantity, and that nothing in the engine needed the base to be an ordinary integer. Running it for a unit of the field, with one new device to handle the prime 2, gives the required count.

Class number one is the condition that every ideal is generated by a single element. It is used at exactly one point, the final appeal to the criterion. The counting theorem itself needs no condition on the class number.

## The connection and the proposed contribution

1. **Read the engine as base-free.** The source manuscript proves Artin's conjecture for integer bases. Its prime-construction proposition produces primes $`p`$ in a prescribed progression with $`p-1=2rQ`$, $`Q`$ a prime larger than $`x^{0.9}`$ and $`r`$ having only mid-sized prime factors; the statement never mentions the base. The base enters only through Kummer fields and a progression of quadratic non-residues.
2. **Kummer fields of a unit.** For a unit, the fact that it is not a $`q`$-th power in $`K(\zeta_q)`$ needs a norm argument in place of a prime divisor. The discriminant of $`K(\zeta_q,\varepsilon^{1/q})`$ is then controlled by $`q^q`$ alone, with no contribution from the base. The manuscript's uniform zero-free strip transfers to these fields by the abelian factorization of Dedekind zeta functions, and its uniform explicit formula bounds the prime ideals splitting completely in them, uniformly in $`q`$.
3. **The residue class minus one modulo the conductor.** Primes $`p\equiv-1\pmod f`$ with $`p\equiv3\pmod4`$ split completely in a real abelian field, because their Frobenius is complex conjugation, and have $`-1`$ as a quadratic non-residue. The unit $`-1`$ therefore handles the prime 2, where the source needs quadratic reciprocity for an integer base. This is the one place where the field must be real.
4. **Assembly and the criterion.** Primes modulo which the unit is a $`q`$-th power for a mid-sized $`q`$ are counted by the splitting bound; for the huge factor $`Q`$, by the size of the norm of $`\varepsilon^{2r}-1`$. A positive proportion of the constructed primes survives, each supplying $`n`$ prime ideals with unit-generated residue fields. Graves's criterion then gives the Euclidean algorithm.

The [detailed proof](proof.md) writes out every step except the three source premises and the published criterion, and lists the five places where it departs from the source.

## Consequences proved in the note

### Every real quadratic principal ideal domain is Euclidean

Conditional on the premises, the two possible exceptions left open by Narkiewicz's theorem do not exist, and the same holds for real cubic and quartic abelian fields of class number one, which the Harper–Murty theorem does not reach. Harper's 2004 proof for $`\mathbb Z[\sqrt{14}]`$ was the first unconditional case of a non-norm-Euclidean real quadratic ring. [Proof](proof.md#euclidean).

### Artin's conjecture for units, in counting form

For every real abelian field and every primitive unit, at least a constant times $`x/(\log x)^2`$ prime ideals of norm at most $`x`$ have residue field generated by $`-1`$ and the unit. This is a lower bound, not Hooley's asymptotic with its density constant. [Proof](proof.md#counting-proof).

### Fibonacci primitive roots

Shanks asked in 1972 whether infinitely many primes have a primitive root $`g`$ with $`g^2\equiv g+1`$. Conditional on the premises, at least a constant times $`x/(\log x)^2`$ primes up to $`x`$ do. The mechanism is that the two roots of $`T^2-T-1`$ modulo $`p`$ multiply to $`-1`$, so when $`p\equiv3\pmod4`$ and the golden ratio together with $`-1`$ generates the residue field, exactly one of the two roots is a primitive root. The infinitude was previously known only under GRH, through Lenstra's and Sander's work. [Proof](proof.md#fibonacci).

The reproducible finite check in the [verification record](verification.md) confirms the mechanism on every prime below one million and finds that the proportion of primes with a Fibonacci primitive root is 0.2664, against Shanks's conjectured density of 0.2657. That comparison is a classical observation included as a sanity check, not a result of this note.

## Prior work and proposed distinction

| Related result | Distinction |
| :--- | :--- |
| [Weinberger 1973](references.md#s6) | Proves the Euclidean conclusion for every field with infinitely many units, assuming the generalized Riemann hypothesis. |
| [Clark–Murty 1995](references.md#s5), [Harper–Murty 2004](references.md#s4) | Unconditional for Galois fields of class number one with unit rank at least four; unit rank one to three excluded. |
| [Harper 2004](references.md#s3) | Unconditional for the single ring $`\mathbb Z[\sqrt{14}]`$, through a computation of admissible primes. |
| [Narkiewicz 2007](references.md#s7) | All but at most two real quadratic fields of class number one, and all but at most one normal cubic field, are Euclidean. |
| [Lenstra 1977](references.md#s8), [Sander 1990](references.md#s12) | Artin's conjecture for units and the Fibonacci density, under GRH. |
| [OpenAI manuscript 029](references.md#s1) | Supplies the machinery and treats integer bases; its text does not mention units, Euclidean rings, Weinberger or Shanks. |

The searches recorded in the [verification record](verification.md#novelty) did not locate an unconditional Euclidean theorem for all real quadratic fields of class number one, nor an unconditional infinitude result for Fibonacci primitive roots, in the sources examined. That is a limited search result, not a certificate that nobody has observed these consequences.

The note does not claim novelty for Graves's criterion, for Hooley's three-range structure, or for the large-prime-factor device of Gupta–Murty and Heath-Brown. Imaginary abelian fields with infinitely many units, Euclidean ideal classes, and the asymptotic density of primes with a given unit as primitive root are outside the statements proved.

## Interpretation and limits

- Everything is conditional on two unformalized manuscripts: family 029 through Hypotheses A, B and C, and family 011 through Hypothesis B. Hypothesis B alone implies that at least a constant times $`x/(\log x)^2`$ primes $`p\le2x`$ have a prime factor of $`p-1`$ exceeding $`x^{0.9}`$, which is far beyond the published record. No theorem forbids this; the verification record lists it as a plausibility consideration.
- The count is a lower bound of order $`x/(\log x)^2`$, not an asymptotic.
- The hypothesis that the field is real is used once, in the choice of residue class; class number one is used once, in the criterion.
- No Lean formalization of the downstream argument exists, and no external specialist has reviewed it.
